# A structural ambiguity in the easy-arithmetic diagnostic

Recorded 2 October 2026 while the fixed control generations were running, before inspecting their outcomes. This adds a caution about prediction equivalence; it changes no data, source roles, learned parameter, intervention, primary metric or generation.

In all 81 primary and all 81 coupled-source records, the first digit of the source's starting operand a equals the first digit of its mathematical intermediate z. This follows from the no-borrow/no-carry ±1 decomposition with units 4, 6 and 8. Consequently,

`g_r(int(first_digit(a_source) + remaining_digits(z_recipient)))`

equals the proposed leading-intermediate-digit prediction on every pair. This alternative inserts a source input digit directly into the recipient intermediate-like number; it differs from the already recorded prediction that first replaces a digit of the recipient's initial operand and then performs f_r.

Even consistent agreement with the coarse prediction, including across the two qualified sources, would therefore not identify a computed source intermediate digit. Report this exact collision and avoid claiming that the factorial grid distinguishes these two hypotheses. The original isolated subspace success is more discriminating on this point: its source starting operands 62 and 47 have leading digits different from z=56. That remains one adaptively noticed example with failed sensitivity to the source's units digit, not a robust mechanism.

A future test of computed leading-digit transfer needs competent sources whose operand-leading-digit and intermediate-leading-digit predictions disagree, with comparable source and recipient controls. Difficulty calibration must not silently remove that diagnostic contrast.
