# Focused literature check during the hidden-value experiment

1 October 2026. These are reports from primary sources, not new results from this project. The earlier ten-source comparison remains in the parent project's `docs/RELATED-WORK.md`. This update is not an exhaustive novelty review.

- [Cheng et al., Can LLMs Reason Abstractly Over Math Word Problems Without CoT?](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.723/): re-read the locally saved final paper, Appendix B.3 and §8. Its two-operator patching analysis uses Qwen2.5-7B and 14B; the authors exclude Llama3-8B from that analysis because of poor task accuracy. Its interpretability evaluation is limited to single-step generation. This supports checking competence separately from mechanism, while showing that two-operation arithmetic interchange is already close prior work. Our complete fresh-answer assay and crossed source controls need their own evidence; the task alone is not novel.
- [Singh et al., Detecting Hidden Chain-of-Thought in Large Language Models with Linguistic, Behavioral, and Mechanistic Indicators](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.29956v1): inspected the abstract, methods, setup and results. The August 2026 preprint evaluates Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 and its Thinking counterpart. It reports that Instruct follows no-CoT instructions more readily. Its main construct is behavioral alignment with CoT versus no-CoT prompt conditions, not observation of a hidden trace. Its mechanistic component is sparse and mixed in sign. This is relevant to choosing a suitable answer-only checkpoint; it does not establish donor-value transfer or replace our own baseline checks.
- [Mechanistic Interpretability of Chain-of-Thought Reasoning via Sequential Activation Patching](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22332v1): inspected intervention methods, experimental scope and limitations. The August 2026 preprint evaluates 32 SVAMP problems, moving source CoT head activations to an answer position and testing combined head interventions. The authors explicitly limit intervention metrics to the first answer token and acknowledge overlapping formatting, grounding and numeric-generation functions. Our complete counterfactual output and donor-value/answer dissociations address a different question; single-token improvement is insufficient for that claim.

The official [Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-FP8 model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-FP8/blob/main/README.md) specifies the non-thinking checkpoint used here. Kernel feasibility was established on the actual GPU. Neither benchmark results nor a model-card description count as evidence that this checkpoint passes our arithmetic assay.

Framing constraint: answering without written steps is an observed output condition. It does not by itself establish an internally serial chain of thought, an explicit numeric register, or a unique algorithm. Selective intervention results would support the stated task-level causal abstraction within the tested conditions.

Recheck on 2 October: the [2026 global-workspace study](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html), especially “Mechanistic Localization,” is a particularly close precedent. It constructs mean-difference interventions for arithmetic intermediates in Opus 4.5 and measures answer-logit shifts across layers. It also tests a shared concept swap across multiple downstream factual questions and an English-concept intervention affecting a Chinese answer. Consequently, neither latent intermediate substitution nor reuse across downstream functions is new in general. Our possible contribution must rest on the exact small-model, complete-generation, control and validation evidence; those outcomes remain pending.
